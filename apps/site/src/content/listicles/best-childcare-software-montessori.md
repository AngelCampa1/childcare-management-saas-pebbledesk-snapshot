Montessori schools face a software problem that most childcare management vendors haven't solved: the curriculum documentation requirements are different enough from standard preschool that generic childcare platforms don't work for them, but the state licensing compliance and billing requirements are identical to every other licensed childcare program.

The result is that most Montessori programs end up with two systems: a Montessori-specific platform for curriculum documentation and a standard childcare compliance platform for licensing and billing. The question is which tools to pair together, not which single tool solves everything.

This comparison covers the realistic options, starting with the tools that matter most for each side of this requirement.

### What makes Montessori documentation different

**Multi-age classrooms:** A Montessori Children's House typically enrolls 3- to 6-year-olds in the same classroom. Ratio tracking needs to account for the mixed ages correctly; most states apply the most restrictive ratio for the youngest age group present, though state rules vary.

**Work-cycle documentation:** Montessori guides document what each child worked on during the three-hour work cycle. This is structured around the Montessori material sequence; practical life, sensorial, language, mathematics, cultural subjects, not around a standardized curriculum checklist. Generic activity logging in childcare software doesn't map to this structure.

**Portfolio-based assessment:** Progress is documented through observation records and portfolio work rather than standardized developmental milestone checklists. Parent-facing communication reflects individual child observations rather than whether the child "achieved" a milestone on schedule.

**Parent communication philosophy:** Sharing a Brightwheel-style daily report that lists "circle time, outdoor play, snack" with a checklist of activities runs counter to how Montessori programs communicate about child progress. Observation-based communication that describes what the child chose to work on and what the guide noticed is the expected format.

### Transparent Classroom: the right starting point for Montessori documentation

Transparent Classroom built its product specifically for Montessori schools. The curriculum framework maps to the Montessori material sequence. Observation recording is tied to lessons and presentations rather than developmental milestones. Parent communication shares observations rather than checklists.

The limitation is operational: Transparent Classroom handles the Montessori curriculum side, not the licensed childcare compliance side. Ratio tracking, staff credential monitoring, incident reporting, CACFP documentation, and state licensing compliance require a second platform. Most Montessori schools using Transparent Classroom are also using something else for compliance.

### PebbleDesk as the compliance complement

PebbleDesk handles the operational compliance layer that Transparent Classroom doesn't: ratio tracking for multi-age rooms, staff credential expiration monitoring, CACFP meal tracking, subsidy billing for CCDF families, and attendance-linked billing. At {{plan.center_starter.priceLabel}}, the cost of running PebbleDesk alongside Transparent Classroom is lower than running a combined compliance-and-curriculum platform that handles neither side well.

The division of responsibilities is clean: Transparent Classroom for everything Montessori guides put into the curriculum documentation. PebbleDesk for everything the licensing inspector asks about.

### Why generic platforms don't fully serve Montessori

Procare and Brightwheel can handle the compliance and billing side. Procare is more expensive; Brightwheel is stronger on parent communication. Neither has Montessori-specific curriculum documentation. Schools that use either platform for compliance still need Transparent Classroom or a similar tool for curriculum documentation.

Famly is closer to the Montessori ethos on parent communication; observation-based documentation rather than checklist formats, but is not a Montessori curriculum platform. The US-specific compliance gaps also require supplementation.

### Practical recommendation for Montessori programs

For a licensed Montessori school in the US: Transparent Classroom for curriculum documentation plus PebbleDesk for compliance and billing. The cost is predictable, the responsibilities are clearly divided, and neither platform tries to be something it isn't.

For a Montessori school that is purely private-pay, doesn't take subsidy, and has simple compliance requirements: Transparent Classroom may be sufficient without a second compliance platform if the state licensing documentation requirements can be handled through manual processes. This depends significantly on the state's licensing audit frequency and the program's appetite for documentation risk.

For a Montessori school with significant CCDF subsidy enrollment: the billing platform becomes more critical. PebbleDesk's subsidy billing handles the mixed-funding structure that Transparent Classroom doesn't touch.
