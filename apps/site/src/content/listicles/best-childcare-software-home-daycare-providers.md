## Who this comparison is for

This page is specifically for licensed family childcare home (FCH) operators: providers who hold a state license to operate an in-home childcare program and, in many cases, serve children funded through DHS vouchers or CCDF (Child Care and Development Fund) subsidies.

This is a different situation than the informal home daycare operator watching neighborhood children under the family childcare exemption. Licensed FCH operators carry the same documentation obligations as licensed childcare centers: attendance records, ratio compliance, subsidy billing reconciliation, and audit-ready documentation for state licensing visits. The child count is smaller. The compliance requirements are not.

Most childcare platforms are built for centers with 50-150 children, multiple staff, and administrative personnel. FCH operators are running a solo business from their home and need software that handles licensing compliance without the overhead built for a center.

### The FCH licensing context

Family childcare home licensing requirements vary significantly by state, but most share these obligations:

**Ratio compliance.** Most states set FCH ratios at 1:6 or 1:8, with additional restrictions on infants or mixed-age groups. A licensed FCH operator must maintain these ratios throughout the day and have documentation available if a licensing officer asks.

**Attendance records.** Daily arrival and departure times, typically required for 90 days or more. These records must be organized and presentable: not reconstructed from memory.

**Subsidy billing documentation.** FCH operators billing DHS vouchers or CCDF must track authorized hours, attendance against authorization, monthly reimbursements received, and any discrepancy between what was billed and what was paid.

These are not optional documentation practices. They are the records a state licensing officer reviews during an inspection and that subsidy agencies request during compliance reviews.

### Why pricing visibility is different for FCH operators

Most childcare software pricing is built around center-scale programs. At center scale, 50, 80, 100 children, pricing can be negotiated around enrollment, classrooms, implementation scope, and payment volume.

At FCH scale, 6, 8, 12 children, quote-required pricing creates a different problem: a solo operator may spend sales-call time before knowing whether the platform fits the budget at all.

### Playground: right tool for private-pay FCH providers

Playground is the right choice for FCH providers who are private-pay, not billing state subsidy, can verify the written price or no-cost state access, and want digital records without committing to a full compliance system.

The product does digital check-in, parent communication, basic invoicing, and payment tracking cleanly. For a non-licensed or private-pay licensed FCH, that covers the operational needs without cost that isn't justified by the size of the program.

Where Playground fails licensed FCH operators is subsidy billing. DHS voucher tracking requires manual work outside the platform. Attendance records are basic rather than audit-formatted. Ratio tracking doesn't exist. If a state licensing officer asks for 90 days of ratio documentation, Playground's check-in logs require significant manual preparation to present in the format inspectors expect.

For licensed FCH operators billing state subsidy, Playground's limitations create manual work that negates the cost savings over PebbleDesk.

### Brightwheel: designed for centers, not solo FCH operators

Brightwheel built its product for licensed childcare centers: multi-staff programs with administrative personnel who can manage a more complex system. The parent communication features (photo sharing, daily activity logs, messaging) are strong.

For a solo FCH operator, these strengths don't resolve the core problems. Pricing isn't published and the sales process is designed for center buyers. Buyers should request a written quote and compare it against tools with published FCH pricing. The compliance tools for subsidy billing and FCH ratio tracking are not built for this use case. The product complexity designed for multi-staff centers creates overhead for a solo operator.

There's a version of Brightwheel that makes sense for an FCH operator: one where parent communication is the top priority, subsidy volume is low, and the operator has tolerance for a sales process and opaque pricing. For most FCH operators comparing on compliance and cost, the fit is weaker than Playground or PebbleDesk.

### Paper records and state forms: the default most FCH operators use

Most licensed FCH operators currently handle compliance with paper records and state-required forms. This is legal, familiar, and costs nothing.

The problem surfaces during licensing audits and subsidy billing reconciliation. A state licensing officer requesting 90 days of attendance records and ratio documentation will receive a stack of paper logs that must be organized and cross-referenced on the spot. Software generates these reports in minutes.

For subsidy billing, tracking DHS voucher amounts, authorized hours, attendance, and monthly reimbursements in paper records is time-consuming and error-prone. Reimbursement clawbacks, where a subsidy agency determines they overpaid because attendance documentation didn't match billing, are a real consequence of reconciliation errors in paper systems.

Paper records are viable for non-licensed, private-pay FCH operators with low compliance burden. For licensed FCH operators billing subsidy, the time cost and audit risk increasingly justify even a modest software investment.

Solo FCH operators often do not have the IT capacity or the program scale to justify that level of setup complexity.
