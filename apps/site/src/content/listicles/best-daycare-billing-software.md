## Why billing is where childcare software earns or loses trust

In a licensed center, billing carries the highest error cost of any administrative task. A missed late fee creates an awkward parent conversation. A subsidy reconciliation error shows up as a reimbursement clawback from a state agency, sometimes months after the fact. An invoice that doesn't match attendance records becomes a problem during an audit.

Most childcare directors manage two billing tracks at once: private-pay families on tuition auto-pay, and subsidized children whose payments come from state agencies as voucher reimbursements. Software that handles one often handles the other poorly.

This comparison covers five platforms on billing only: not parent communication, not photo sharing, not staff scheduling.

### What licensed centers need from billing software

For a licensed center with mixed enrollment, "billing" covers two distinct problems:


### Tuition billing (private-pay)

Auto-pay setup from a bank account or card on file, invoice generation with accurate rates and schedules, sibling discount rules, deposit collection and tracking, late fee automation when payments don't come through, and a payment history that satisfies parents and auditors alike.



### Subsidy billing (state-funded children)

Tracking which enrolled children hold active DHS vouchers or CCDF certificates, matching daily attendance records against voucher terms (authorized hours, days), reconciling monthly reimbursement payments from the state against what was billed, and generating the attendance and billing documentation that subsidy agencies request during compliance reviews.


The distinction matters because most platforms handle tuition billing competently. Subsidy reconciliation is where the category splits.

The limitation is that PebbleDesk is a newer product. For complex multi-site operations or programs with unusual subsidy agency requirements, that experience gap may matter.

### Procare: most established subsidy billing, quote required

Procare is the legacy platform in this category, and subsidy billing is one of its strongest features. The system has handled CCDF billing, DHS voucher reconciliation, and state-specific subsidy programs for long enough that most edge cases are accounted for.

The problems are pricing visibility and architecture. Procare does not publish standard pricing, so directors should request a quote and verify which billing, subsidy, and implementation services are included. The platform was built as a desktop application, and while cloud access exists, the interface reflects its origins. Staff training takes more time than with newer platforms.

For large centers or multi-site operators where subsidy billing volume justifies the implementation effort, Procare is the most capable option. For a single-site center under 50 children, buyers should compare the quote against published-price alternatives unless specific subsidy agency requirements force Procare.

### Brightwheel: tuition billing done well, subsidy reconciliation limited

Brightwheel's billing tools for private-pay families are well-built. Auto-pay setup is straightforward, payment reminders reduce the number of conversations directors have to initiate, and the parent-facing billing experience is polished.

Where Brightwheel falls short for licensed programs is subsidy reconciliation. Centers billing DHS vouchers or CCDF subsidies should ask Brightwheel to demonstrate voucher tracking, reimbursement reconciliation, and subsidy agency reporting before assuming those workflows replace spreadsheets or state portal work. That manual layer adds administrative time and introduces the reconciliation risk that purpose-built subsidy tools are designed to eliminate.

Brightwheel pricing is not published. Directors should request a quote, confirm the billing tier, and ask specifically how subsidy reconciliation works before committing. A center with significant subsidy revenue is weighing subsidy reconciliation capability against Brightwheel's stronger parent communication features.

If the center's priority is private-pay family billing and parent engagement, Brightwheel is worth evaluating. If subsidy billing is a significant operational need, the limitations create ongoing manual work.

### Kangarootime: solid private-pay billing, limited subsidy

Kangarootime is billing-forward relative to some competitors: auto-pay, invoicing, and sibling discount rules are core features rather than add-ons. For private-pay centers, it's a competent option.

Subsidy billing is limited, and ratio tracking is manual rather than automated. For licensed programs where compliance documentation and subsidy reconciliation are operational priorities, Kangarootime's gaps become significant. Because standard pricing is not public, buyers should verify the quote against the compliance features licensed programs need most.

### Playground: basic billing, private-pay only

Playground standard pricing is not published, though no-cost access may be available for qualifying providers through some state partnership programs.

The billing tools cover the basics, invoices, payment tracking, but late fee automation is absent, subsidy billing requires manual tracking, and deposit management is not included. For small private-pay programs, Playground works. For licensed centers with complex billing requirements, it's not positioned for this use case.
