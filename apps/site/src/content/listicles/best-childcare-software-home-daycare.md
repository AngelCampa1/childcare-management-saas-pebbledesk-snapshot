## Who this comparison is for

Home daycare operators, whether you run a family childcare home with 4 children or a licensed in-home program with 12, have different needs than center directors managing 50-100 children. Software built for large centers has more complexity than a home program needs and costs more than the economics justify.

This comparison covers four options that make sense at home daycare scale, including the option most operators start with: nothing.

### The real question: licensed or not?

The software decision turns on licensing status and billing type.

Non-licensed, private-pay home daycares watching 1-3 children can operate with a spreadsheet or paper records. Operational and compliance risk is low at this scale.

Licensed family childcare homes, especially those billing state subsidy vouchers (CCDF, DHS, state-specific programs), face a different situation. State licensing officers expect clean attendance records. Subsidy agencies require documentation formats that paper trails are hard to produce quickly. One disorganized audit creates hours of work and potential reimbursement delays.

For licensed operators, the question is which software matches compliance requirements without overwhelming a one-person operation.

### What state licensing audits require

A state licensing visit typically involves attendance records showing daily arrival and departure times, ratio documentation showing staff-to-child counts throughout each day, and billing records for subsidized children that match attendance.

Paper records can satisfy these requirements if consistently maintained. In practice, home daycare operators run the whole program, caring for children, managing parent communication, handling billing, and paper records slip when the day is busy.

Software that records check-in and check-out automatically and generates these reports on demand is insurance against a stressful audit.
