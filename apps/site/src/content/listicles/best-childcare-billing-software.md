### Quick recommendation

For licensed centers comparing childcare or daycare billing software in 2026, PebbleDesk Center is the best pick when private-pay tuition collection and subsidy reconciliation both matter. Brightwheel is stronger for parent-facing auto-pay in mostly private-pay programs. Procare fits larger subsidy-heavy operators that can handle quote-required procurement and setup time.

### Why billing software choice matters

Licensed childcare centers billing state subsidy don't have a billing problem: they have two billing problems running simultaneously. Private-pay families pay tuition on a schedule. CCDF and DHS-funded children generate reimbursements from state agencies, tied to daily attendance records and authorized hours. Most billing software handles one of these tracks. Centers with mixed enrollment end up managing the other track in a spreadsheet.

That distinction, between software built for private-pay tuition and software built for multi-funding-stream billing, is the thing worth understanding before evaluating any platform in this category.

This comparison covers five platforms on billing specifically, with particular attention to how each handles CCDF and DHS subsidy reconciliation alongside private-pay tuition. The daycare billing software comparison covers simpler private-pay scenarios in more detail. This page focuses on centers where subsidy billing is a meaningful operational requirement.

### How we evaluated

We evaluated childcare billing software against the work that causes billing errors in real centers: automated billing, tuition collection, family co-pay calculation, subsidy reconciliation, attendance-linked documentation, late fees, price transparency, and implementation burden.

We weighted each platform by billing fit, not by brand size. A private-pay billing tool can still be a poor fit for a center where CCDF or DHS subsidy revenue drives cash flow. Published prices and existing platform documentation counted more than sales language. When pricing is not public, the page says so instead of treating estimates as quotes.

### What multi-funding-stream billing actually requires

For a licensed center with mixed enrollment, billing involves two parallel systems that have to reconcile against each other:


### Private-pay billing

Auto-pay from a card or bank account on file, invoice generation with correct rates per enrolled schedule, sibling discount rules, deposit collection and tracking, late fee automation when payments fail, and a payment history that satisfies parents and auditors.



### Subsidy billing (CCDF/DHS)

Tracking which enrolled children hold active voucher authorizations, validating authorized hours against actual attendance, generating the attendance documentation the subsidy agency requires, reconciling monthly reimbursement payments against what was billed, and calculating the family co-pay: the gap between the voucher amount and the full tuition rate, which the family still owes.


The co-pay calculation is where most workarounds break down. If the voucher covers $600 of a $750 monthly rate, the family owes $150. That $150 has to be invoiced to the parent separately from the subsidy billing. Centers without software that understands this split end up doing that calculation manually for every subsidized child every billing period.

### Procare: most established subsidy billing, quote-required pricing

Procare is the historical standard for subsidy billing in childcare software. CCDF billing, DHS voucher reconciliation, and state-specific subsidy program variants have been core features for long enough that most edge cases are accounted for. Reporting tools satisfy both subsidy agencies and state licensing auditors.

The quote process is the primary comparison barrier. Procare's current public pricing page requires a quote, so directors need a sales process before comparing it against published flat-rate plans like PebbleDesk Center Starter. The platform's desktop-first architecture means that while cloud access exists, the interface reflects its origins. Staff training takes more time, and implementation is more involved than newer platforms.

For large programs or multi-site operators where subsidy billing volume justifies the quote process and the edge-case coverage matters, Procare remains the most capable option. Single-site centers under 50 children should compare the quoted price and implementation effort against lighter alternatives.

### Brightwheel: strong tuition billing, limited subsidy reconciliation

Brightwheel's billing tools for private-pay families are well-built. Auto-pay setup is straightforward, payment reminders work, and the parent-facing experience is polished.

Where Brightwheel falls short for mixed-funding centers is CCDF and DHS subsidy reconciliation. Centers with significant subsidy revenue should ask Brightwheel to demonstrate voucher tracking, reimbursement reconciliation, and subsidy agency reporting before assuming those workflows replace spreadsheets or state portal work. That creates the double-entry problem that purpose-built subsidy billing addresses.

Brightwheel pricing is not published, and payment service fees vary by program. A center with meaningful subsidy enrollment should compare its quote against the subsidy reconciliation capability it still needs outside Brightwheel, not just the parent communication features. For primarily private-pay programs, that may be a reasonable trade. For mixed-funding centers, it typically isn't.

### Kangarootime: solid private-pay, limited subsidy

Kangarootime is billing-forward relative to some competitors: auto-pay, invoicing, and sibling discount rules are core features rather than afterthoughts. For private-pay centers, it's a capable option if the quoted pricing and workflow fit the program.

Subsidy billing is limited, and ratio tracking is manual. For licensed programs where CCDF reconciliation and compliance documentation are operational priorities, Kangarootime's gaps are significant. The workflow tradeoff does not differentiate clearly from Brightwheel for mixed-funding scenarios.

### Playground: basic billing, private-pay only

Playground standard pricing is not published, though no-cost access may be available for qualifying providers through some state partnership programs. The billing tools cover invoices and payment tracking for private-pay families.

Late fee automation is absent, and subsidy billing requires tracking outside the platform. For small programs with straightforward private-pay billing, Playground works. For licensed centers billing CCDF or DHS subsidy, it's not positioned for this use case.
