## Charlotte childcare licensing overview

Charlotte is the largest childcare market in North Carolina, with approximately 420 licensed establishments in Mecklenburg County and around 700 across the metro area. The North Carolina Division of Child Development and Early Education licenses all childcare centers in the state under 10A NCAC 09, with a distinctive feature that separates North Carolina from most other states: a mandatory star-rated license system that applies to every licensed provider.

For Charlotte directors, the compliance picture includes DCDEE licensing and star-rating maintenance, NC CCAP billing through Child Care Resources Inc., and increasingly, the NC Pre-K program requirements for centers that participate in state-funded pre-kindergarten.

## The NC star-rated license system

North Carolina assigns every licensed childcare center a star rating from 1 to 5 at the time of initial licensing, based on factors beyond the minimum health and safety standards. Star ratings factor in teacher education levels, staff-to-child ratio compliance history, curriculum documentation, and ongoing staff professional development.

The star rating matters operationally for Charlotte directors in three concrete ways. First, NC Pre-K contracts represent substantial revenue for participating centers and are generally only available to centers with higher star ratings. Second, families in Mecklenburg County can filter childcare provider searches on the DCDEE website by star rating, making higher ratings a marketing differentiator. Third, NC CCAP subsidy rates are tiered by star rating, so higher-rated centers receive higher subsidy reimbursement per enrolled child.

Software that maintains organized documentation of staff education records, professional development hours, and curriculum implementation supports star-rating maintenance as a by-product of daily operations, not as a separate documentation effort.

## NC CCAP billing through Child Care Resources Inc.

Charlotte-area families qualifying for NC CCAP work through Child Care Resources Inc. (CCRI), which administers Mecklenburg County's share of North Carolina's CCDF subsidy. Providers with NC CCAP families must submit attendance records through the NC FAST system or CCRI's provider portal, depending on the program type.

The documentation requirements for NC CCAP billing include daily attendance records, child eligibility verification documentation, and provider agreements with CCRI. Software that produces attendance records in NC FAST-compatible format reduces the manual re-entry that otherwise occurs when attendance logged in your center management system must be re-entered into the state portal.

## Charlotte market characteristics

Charlotte's financial services and technology employment base, anchored by Bank of America, Wells Fargo, and a growing tech sector, creates consistent demand for full-time infant and toddler care from dual-income professional families. The SouthPark, Ballantyne, and University City corridors have high concentrations of these households with strong private-pay demand.

West Charlotte, Steele Creek, and parts of northeast Mecklenburg County have higher concentrations of CCAP-eligible families and more complex subsidy billing requirements. Multi-site operators working across the county's economic geography need software that handles star-rating documentation, NC CCAP billing, and NC Pre-K reporting in a unified system.

## What Charlotte directors should evaluate in software

Three evaluation priorities for North Carolina's regulatory environment:

Star-rating documentation support: does the software maintain staff education records, professional development tracking, and curriculum documentation in a format that supports DCDEE star-rating review? These are not incidental features; they affect reimbursement rates and NC Pre-K eligibility.

NC FAST compatibility: request your current NC CCAP provider documentation from CCRI and verify that any software you evaluate produces attendance records compatible with NC FAST submission requirements.

Ratio and group size monitoring: North Carolina caps both ratios and group sizes. Does the software track both in real time, not just ratios?
