## What CCDF is and why documentation matters

Every state CCDF-funded subsidy program ties reimbursement to documented attendance. The provider bills for days a child was present (or for allowed absence days within the monthly cap), and the state reimburses based on the authorization and rate code on file. The audit trail that connects billing to attendance is the entire basis for a defensible claim.

Attendance documentation is where most CCDF audit findings live. The rules are not technically complex; sign in, sign out, signature, date, but the discipline required to do this correctly every day for every child is real. A center with 40 subsidy children requires 80 signatures a day, 1,600 per month, 19,200 per year. Gaps accumulate quickly when the capture process relies on memory and end-of-day paperwork.

The goal of a CCDF-ready attendance system is simple: every billed day has a clean record, captured same-day, with the verification the state requires.

## What attendance records CCDF actually requires

The required data elements are consistent across most states:

**Child identification.** The full name and state-assigned identifier of the child whose attendance is being recorded. First name alone is insufficient if multiple children share it.

**Date of service.** The specific calendar date. This must match the billed date exactly on the claim.

**Time in and time out.** Captured at the moment of arrival and departure, not estimated at day's end. Time stamps are what state auditors reconcile against the authorized hours.

**Verification.** Signature or electronic confirmation by a parent, guardian, or authorized pickup adult. Same-day verification is the standard.

**Absence records.** For absent children whose slot is still billable, document the absence date and a reason code. Most states require this for any absence included in the billing claim.

**Provider verification.** A staff member's confirmation that the child was present and supervised. This is sometimes captured automatically when check-in flows through a staff-operated device.

States layer additional requirements on top of these basics. Some require absence reason codes from a specific list. Some require monthly attendance summary reports in a specific format. Always reference your state's CCDF provider handbook for the specific rules.

## Common documentation gaps that delay reimbursement

Three attendance-specific gaps cause most CCDF claim problems.

**Missed sign-outs at end of day.** A parent picks up in a rush and forgets to sign out. The record is incomplete. If this happens often, auditors flag the pattern and claims slow down. Prevention: staff verification with a same-day note when the parent misses the signature.

**Retroactive signatures.** Records signed days or weeks later at the office rather than same-day at drop-off and pickup. Auditors consider these weak documentation, and repeated use triggers improper payment findings. Prevention: capture at the moment, not at month-end.

**Untracked absences.** A child is absent for 6 days in a month with no absence records, and the center bills for all 22 enrolled days. The billed days exceed the attended days plus allowable absences, an immediate reconciliation problem. Prevention: absence codes are entered the same day the absence occurs.

A subtler gap: inconsistent identifiers. A child entered as "Michael J." in the attendance log but billed as "Michael John" on the claim. The records technically match, but automated reconciliation flags the discrepancy and slows the claim. Use full legal names everywhere.

## How to build a system that stays audit-ready

The operational target: a director who can produce 12 months of CCDF-billable attendance documentation within 10 minutes when a state auditor asks.

Getting there requires three things.

**Capture at the moment, not after.** The attendance record exists when the parent signs in, not when an admin staff member compiles records at month-end. Same-day capture is the single most important discipline.

**One record per day per child, not separate systems.** Attendance, billing, and audit records should all reference the same underlying row. When systems are separate, reconciliation becomes manual.

**Proactive alerts for gaps.** The system should flag missing signatures, incomplete sign-outs, or unrecorded absences same-day, not at month-end. Directors should see the alert and resolve it within 24 hours.

These properties apply whether the records are captured on paper or electronically. The advantage of electronic capture is enforcement: required fields can't be skipped, and time stamps are automatic. The advantage of paper is simplicity. Most modern CCDF-eligible providers use electronic capture with paper backup for specific audit-sample days.
