## The calculation most directors get wrong

When a director evaluates childcare management software, the number on the screen is the software cost. What's rarely written down is the cost of not having software; the admin hours, the billing errors, the subsidy claim losses, and the licensing risk from incomplete records.

The comparison isn't "software cost vs. zero." It's "software cost vs. what you're already spending without knowing it."

Here's how to build the real comparison.

## Step 1: Price your admin hours correctly

This is where most ROI calculations fall apart. Directors undervalue their own time because it feels like "just part of the job." But if you're spending three hours every Tuesday reconciling attendance against invoices, that time has a real cost; whether you're calculating it or not.

Use your effective hourly rate. For a director earning $55,000/year, that's approximately $26/hour. For an owner-operator who handles administration personally, use your actual cost-of-time: what would you pay someone else to do what you're doing?

**Admin hours commonly saved by childcare software:**

Automated billing runs: centers generating invoices manually typically spend 2-4 hours per week on invoice creation, adjustment, and parent communication. Software with automated billing tied to attendance cuts this to 20-30 minutes of review.

Subsidy attendance reconciliation: preparing documentation for CCDF, Head Start, or state subsidy programs from manual records takes 1-3 hours per week for centers with 10+ subsidy children. Software that formats this automatically cuts that to near zero.

Staff credential tracking: managing CPR expiration dates, training requirements, and licensing renewals from a spreadsheet consumes 30-60 minutes per week. A system with automated alerts eliminates the active tracking time entirely.

Enrollment paperwork: digital enrollment reduces the per-child intake time from 45-60 minutes of data entry to 15-20 minutes of review.

Add these up and you'll typically find 5-10 hours per week in recoverable admin time for a center with 40-60 enrolled children.

## Step 2: Calculate billing error exposure

Manual billing systems create errors through predictable mechanisms: wrong rate applied to a child who aged into a new age group, attendance record that doesn't match the invoice period, subsidy co-pay that wasn't collected, or a rate increase applied to some families but missed for others.

For private-pay families, billing errors typically net out over time; some you catch, some you absorb. The real exposure is on the subsidy side.

A single disallowed subsidy attendance record represents the full daily rate for that child on that day, typically $50-$80 for full-time care, depending on your state and age group. An audit that finds systematic documentation errors across 30 days and 15 subsidy children is a $22,500-$36,000 recoupment event.

Even absent an audit, routine subsidy claim processing rejects individual attendance records for documentation issues. A center losing $200/month in rejected claims due to documentation gaps is losing $2,400/year; often without realizing it because rejections come months after the fact and are easy to attribute to the family rather than the record.

## Step 3: Calculate subsidy reconciliation time

Subsidy billing is fundamentally different from private-pay billing. Different payer, different timing, different documentation requirements, different audit exposure. Centers managing subsidy manually; preparing attendance summaries, verifying eligibility against state rosters, tracking claim status, typically spend 2-6 hours per month per state subsidy program on pure administrative reconciliation.

For a center with children in two subsidy programs spending four hours monthly on reconciliation at $26/hour, that's $1,248/year in recoverable admin time from subsidy reconciliation alone.

## The ROI formula

Put the four components together:

**Annual admin hours saved** = weekly hours saved × $hourly rate × 52

**Annual billing error reduction** = estimated monthly billing gap × 12 (start conservative: $100-200/month for a typical center)

**Annual subsidy reconciliation savings** = monthly hours × $hourly rate × 12

**Licensing risk reduction** = harder to quantify, but a single licensing citation typically costs $250-$2,500 in fines plus remediation time; incomplete records are the most common finding

Sum those four numbers. That's your annual benefit from software.

**ROI = (Annual benefit − Annual software cost) ÷ Annual software cost**

For a center with 50 enrolled children and 15 subsidy families:

- Admin time: 6 hours/week × $26/hour × 52 = $8,112
- Billing error reduction: $150/month × 12 = $1,800
- Subsidy reconciliation: 3 hours/month × $26/hour × 12 = $936
- Licensing risk: $500 (conservative estimate)

Total annual benefit: $11,348

At $250/month ($3,000/year) for software, ROI = ($11,348 − $3,000) ÷ $3,000 = **278% ROI**

## What changes this math

The ROI is larger if you're currently on paper or Excel. Transitioning from a manual system captures the full admin time savings. Centers already using basic software see more modest improvements, primarily in billing accuracy and subsidy documentation.

The ROI is smaller for very small centers (under 20 children) where admin time is proportionally lower, or for centers with minimal subsidy enrollment where the biggest error exposure doesn't apply.

The ROI is larger than you'd expect for multi-site operators, because administrative overhead doesn't scale linearly; two locations don't require double the software cost, but they do require close to double the admin time when managed manually.

## Before you sign

Run this calculation with your actual numbers before any demo. Write down what you're currently spending in admin time each week. Estimate your billing gap honestly. That's the baseline the software needs to beat, and for most centers, it beats it comfortably.
