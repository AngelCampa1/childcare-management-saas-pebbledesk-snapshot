## The Home Daycare Compliance Challenge

Running a licensed home daycare means meeting the same compliance requirements as a childcare center, with two key differences: you have no administrative staff and your budget is a fraction of a center's.

You are the caregiver, the cook, the cleaner, the administrator, and the compliance officer. The time you spend on paperwork is time taken away from the children or from your own life after hours. Paper sign-in sheets, handwritten incident reports, and spreadsheet billing are functional but slow.

## What State Licensing Requires

### Attendance Records

Every state requires time-stamped attendance records for licensed home daycares. The specific format varies, but the core requirement is the same: you need to document when each child arrives and departs, every day. If a subsidy agency requests attendance verification, your records need to match billing claims.

Digital check-in is the simplest upgrade from paper. A parent taps a button on a tablet or phone at drop-off and pickup. The time stamp is automatic and accurate. No more deciphering handwriting or rounding times at the end of the day.

### Ratio Tracking

Home daycares have strict ratio limits, typically one provider to a set number of children based on the age mix present. If you care for infants, the ratio is tighter. Most states require that you stay within ratio at all times, not just during headcount.

With a small group, ratio tracking can feel unnecessary. But if you have a mixed-age group and a parent drops off early or picks up late, your ratio can shift. Having a system that flags these moments provides documentation in case of a complaint or inspection.

### Incident Documentation

Any injury, illness, or notable behavioral incident requires documentation and parent notification. States set specific timeframes for filing incident reports. A digital incident report with a timestamp is faster to create than a handwritten form and easier to retrieve during an audit.

## Choosing Software That Fits a Home Operation

### Avoid Per-Child Pricing

Per-child pricing models are designed for centers that enroll 50+ children. At $5-10 per child per month, a center with 60 children pays $300-600/month. For a home daycare with 10 children, the same pricing model costs $50-100/month, which is more reasonable but still potentially more than flat-rate alternatives.

### Prioritize Simplicity

You do not need a feature list designed for multi-site operators. You need attendance, basic billing, incident reports, and access to records when an auditor asks. Every extra feature is complexity you have to learn and manage with no admin support.

### Check State-Specific Requirements

Some states have specific electronic record-keeping requirements or approved software lists. Before committing to a platform, verify that it meets your state's particular documentation standards. PebbleDesk is designed to accommodate varying state requirements, but always confirm with your licensing agency.

## The Daily Workflow

A well-designed home daycare software fits into your existing routine without adding steps. Parents check in at drop-off. You note any incidents as they happen. Billing runs at the end of the month based on attendance data already captured. When licensing calls, the records are there.

The entire system should add minutes per day, not hours per week. If the software is taking more time than the paper system it replaced, it is the wrong tool.
