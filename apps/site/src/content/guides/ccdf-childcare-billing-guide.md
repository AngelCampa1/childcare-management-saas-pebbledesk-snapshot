## What CCDF is and why documentation matters

CCDF is the federal funding source for state child care subsidy programs. Every state receives a CCDF block grant from the federal government, adds state matching funds, and runs its own subsidy program under state-specific rules. Names vary; CCAP, voucher programs, state child care assistance, but the federal CCDF requirements anchor the core rules.

For a licensed childcare center, CCDF revenue can be anywhere from 10% to 90% of monthly income. For centers in low-income service areas, subsidy billing is often the largest single revenue line. The documentation discipline required to collect that revenue reliably is the difference between a thin-margin operation and a healthy one.

The core requirement across all CCDF-funded programs: every billed day must be supported by a continuous chain of documentation. Attendance record, authorization document, co-pay record, claim submission. If any link in the chain is missing or inconsistent, the claim is at risk.

## What billing documentation CCDF requires

Five categories of documentation drive almost every CCDF claim reconciliation:

**Attendance records**: Time-stamped sign-in and sign-out for each day billed. Most states require both a parent/guardian signature and a staff verification. Electronic check-in systems that capture this automatically are acceptable in most states as long as the audit trail is preserved.

**Authorization documents**: The state-issued voucher or authorization covering the billed period. Track start and end dates actively. Expired authorizations are a leading cause of claim rejection.

**Co-pay records**: Proof that the family's co-pay was collected at the authorized amount. If co-pays are waived or reduced, the state-authorized waiver must be on file.

**Rate verification**: Documentation that the rate billed matches the state-assigned rate code for the specific child. Rate codes depend on child age, care type (full-day, part-day), and geographic region.

**Claim submission records**: A copy of each filed claim with the submission date, the dates of care claimed, and the reimbursement expected. Keep these for the state-required retention period (typically 5 to 7 years).

## Common documentation gaps that delay reimbursement

Three gaps cause the majority of rejected or delayed CCDF claims.

First: attendance hours that don't match authorization hours. A child authorized for 40 hours per week whose attendance shows 45 hours one week creates an over-claim. The state pays for 40 and kicks back the additional 5. If the provider billed for 45, the claim processes late while the discrepancy is investigated.

Second: missing or late co-pay documentation. State auditors routinely pull co-pay records for a random sample of billed days. If the records are incomplete or signed months after the fact, the claim is flagged for improper payment.

Third: authorizations that expired during the billing period. Providers often catch this too late; after the claim is filed and the state rejects the dates after expiration. Proactive authorization tracking prevents this entirely.

A secondary gap worth mentioning: absence handling. Most states allow a limited number of billable absence days per month. Centers that bill every enrolled day regardless of attendance risk rejections when absence thresholds are exceeded.

## How to build a system that stays audit-ready

A working CCDF system has three properties.

**One source of truth for attendance.** If check-in records live in one system and billing records live in another, reconciliation between them is manual and error-prone. The systems that work best generate billing claims directly from the attendance record, not as a separate data entry step.

**Proactive authorization tracking.** Authorizations should surface on your dashboard 30 days, 14 days, and 7 days before expiration; early enough to confirm renewal status with families. Waiting for the state to reject a claim is too late.

**Automated co-pay reconciliation.** Co-pays collected should reconcile against the authorized amount automatically. Any discrepancy should surface on the same dashboard that shows outstanding claims. Matching co-pays by hand at month-end is how gaps go undetected for months.

The operational goal is simple: by the time a state auditor asks for documentation, the answer is already assembled. No weekend spreadsheet work. No frantic search for a missing co-pay receipt from March.
