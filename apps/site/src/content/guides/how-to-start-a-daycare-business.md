## Licensing first, then facility

New daycare operators typically start with the space and the children they want to serve. That instinct is right, but the sequence that produces a license is: licensing requirements first, then facility, then staff, then families.

State licensing rules set how much square footage you need per child, what credentials your director must hold, which staff-to-child ratios apply by age group, and what records you must maintain from day one. Sign a lease before confirming zoning approval and measuring against state minimums and you may be locked into a space that cannot hit your target licensed capacity.

New centers take 3-6 months from application to first enrollment. That timeline belongs in your financial projections early.

## Step 1: Research your state's licensing requirements

Contact your state's childcare licensing agency directly. State rules update year to year, and outdated information creates real compliance exposure. General guides are not reliable sources for this.

Ask for the current licensing rules document, ratio requirements by age group, required square footage per child (indoor and outdoor), staff qualification standards, and the pre-licensing inspection checklist.

Your state's Child Care Resource and Referral (CCR&R) network is a good first call. CCR&R agencies provide free pre-licensing consultations in many states and can tell you which licensing track applies to your program type.

## Step 2: Choose your program type

Family childcare homes and center-based programs follow separate licensing tracks. The choice affects your ratio rules, director qualification requirements, and facility standards.

Family childcare homes typically serve 6-12 children in a residential setting. Startup costs are lower, but licensed capacity hits a hard ceiling. Center-based programs carry higher fixed costs and more complex licensing requirements, with more room to grow enrollment over time.

A home license and a center license are different documents with different ongoing compliance obligations. Make this decision before you invest in a facility.

## Step 3: Secure your facility

Zoning approval must come before a lease. Childcare centers require specific zoning designations, and many buildings that look suitable do not qualify.

Once zoning is confirmed, measure usable indoor floor space against your state's per-child minimums, commonly 35 square feet indoors and 75 square feet of outdoor play area per child, though your state may differ. Verify kitchen and bathroom requirements for your target licensed capacity.

Get your proposed licensed capacity confirmed in writing before signing anything. A 2,000-square-foot space that can only be licensed for 30 children changes your revenue model significantly if you planned for 50.

## Step 4: Complete licensing application and facility inspection

Submit your application with all required documentation. Missing items generate additional information requests that restart processing timelines.

Inspectors check that your physical space matches the approved floor plan, that safety equipment is in place, and that your director's qualifications are on file. They also review posted policies and your staffing plan against ratio requirements. Any deficiency in the inspection report requires resolution and re-inspection. Do not schedule your opening for the week after your initial inspection.

## Step 5: Hire qualified staff

Every state sets minimum director qualifications and teacher requirements by classroom age group, typically combining an Early Childhood Education credential with a minimum number of supervised experience hours.

All staff must clear background checks through your state's registry before their first day of care. Clearances take 2-6 weeks depending on the state. Start this early. Enrollment cannot begin without cleared staff in place, and ratio compliance obligations start on day one.

## Step 6: Set up your administration systems

Before the first child arrives, you need attendance tracking that produces records you can hand to a licensing officer, an enrollment process that captures all required authorizations, and a subsidy billing setup if you plan to accept CCDF vouchers.

Paper systems are common at launch. The problem is that CCDF billing requires attendance documented by the hour or day depending on your state, and a documentation gap in the first billing cycle means rejected claims your program does not recover. Directors who stood up their records systems quickly at opening told us those same systems became their biggest compliance problem twelve months in.

## Step 7: Begin enrollment

Start your waitlist before you open. Licensing timelines slip. Families ready to enroll before your license arrives mean you reach full enrollment revenue faster after approval.

For subsidy families, verify CCDF eligibility and authorization dates before the first day of care. Claims where care started before an authorization date are not reimbursed. That is unpaid care your program absorbs.

Document every enrollment transaction and subsidy authorization from the first day. Licensing audits look back 2-3 years, and records from month one may be reviewed years after opening.
