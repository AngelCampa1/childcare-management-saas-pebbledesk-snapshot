## New Jersey childcare licensing overview

New Jersey has approximately 2,800 licensed childcare establishments as of 2024, with the largest concentration in the northern NJ/Newark corridor and smaller clusters in Trenton, Camden, and the Atlantic City area. The Department of Children and Families, DCF, licenses childcare centers under N.J.A.C. 3A:52, a detailed regulatory code covering staff ratios, physical environment, staff qualifications, and administrative recordkeeping.

DCF licensing oversight in New Jersey is detailed. Inspectors review administrative records as thoroughly as they review physical conditions, and citation rates for recordkeeping gaps are meaningful. Centers that maintain digital, organized documentation move through inspections faster than those relying on paper logs or spreadsheets that have to be reconstructed at inspection time.

The compliance environment matters for software selection. A tool that works for a Texas center billing one set of subsidy requirements may not produce records in the format a NJ DCF auditor expects.

## Staff-to-child ratios and what they mean for software

NJ N.J.A.C. 3A:52 ratios run from 1:4 for infants under 18 months to 1:15 for school-age children. New Jersey's infant ratio of 1:4 is among the strictest ratios in the country. For a center with an infant room, that ratio sets a hard staffing floor: one teacher for every four infants, at all times.

The 18-month age cutoff for the infant ratio category is worth flagging. A child who turns 18 months transitions from the 1:4 infant ratio to the 1:6 ratio for the 18-35 month group. Software that tracks children only by room and not by individual age creates classification risk, particularly in centers with mixed-age toddler rooms where children straddle the boundary.

Ratio compliance must be documented throughout the operating day. Morning check-in logs satisfy only part of the requirement. Inspectors look at teacher break coverage, room transitions, and end-of-day staffing: the times when ratios are most likely to slip.

## Subsidy billing through NJ CCAP

NJ CCAP, the Child Care Assistance Program, is New Jersey's CCDF-funded childcare subsidy, administered by DCF in coordination with county agencies. Centers accepting CCAP children must be licensed by DCF and maintain attendance records that satisfy DCF's subsidy audit documentation requirements.

Billing is attendance-based. What you submit for CCAP reimbursement must match what your attendance records document. A discrepancy is an audit finding. DCF subsidy audits examine the source records, your daily attendance logs, not just the billing submissions.

Contact your county agency contact and DCF's Office of Child Care Licensing to confirm current provider submission requirements before choosing software. Requirements have changed with NJ's subsidy system updates, and vendor claims about compatibility should be verified against current DCF guidance.

## Seasonal enrollment patterns

Northern NJ centers serving school-age children see summer enrollment drop when school-age kids leave or reduce hours. Centers with licensed summer programs, particularly in urban areas where families rely on licensed care year-round, recover more of this volume than suburban centers.

Before/after school demand returns in September when NJ public schools resume. Infant enrollment in northern NJ runs at high demand year-round relative to available licensed capacity, making infant programs a stable revenue base for centers that can staff them at the 1:4 ratio.

Monthly CCAP payment cycles mean cash flow for subsidy-billing centers depends on clean attendance records submitted on schedule. Late or incomplete attendance documentation delays payment, which is a common cash flow problem for centers that manage attendance manually.

## What software needs to handle in New Jersey

Age-group tracking by individual child, not just by room assignment. NJ's 18-month infant ratio cutoff means a child's specific age determines which ratio applies, and centers with mixed-age toddler rooms need software that tracks this accurately.

Continuous ratio documentation throughout the operating day: not just morning sign-in and afternoon sign-out. DCF inspectors review mid-day and transition-period ratio records.

Attendance records that satisfy NJ CCAP audit documentation standards. Generic attendance exports may not meet the format DCF expects for subsidy verification. Confirm compatibility before committing to a platform.
