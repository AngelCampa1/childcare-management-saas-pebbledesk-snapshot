## Arkansas childcare licensing overview

Arkansas has approximately 700 licensed childcare establishments as of 2024, concentrated in Little Rock, the Fayetteville-Springdale corridor in northwest Arkansas, Fort Smith, and Jonesboro. The Arkansas Department of Human Services Division of Child Care and Early Childhood Education licenses childcare centers under rule 016.06.12, covering staffing ratios, physical environment, staff qualifications, and recordkeeping.

Arkansas has a documentation retention requirement that shapes how directors need to think about software: ratio records must be retained for two years. Not best practices: a legal requirement. That means a center that switched software 18 months ago still needs to produce ratio documentation from before the switch if a DCCECE inspector asks for it. Software that does not export historical records in a retrievable format creates a compliance gap the moment the subscription ends.

Northwest Arkansas is growing faster than the rest of the state, driven in part by Walmart's corporate presence in Bentonville and the surrounding employer ecosystem. Centers in that region are experiencing demand patterns closer to suburban growth markets than the rest of Arkansas.

## Staff-to-child ratios and what they mean for software

Arkansas rule 016.06.12 ratios step from 1:6 for the youngest infants to 1:18 for children four and older. The toddler ratio of 1:9 for 13-24 month-olds sits between the stricter infant requirement and the 2-year-old ratio, creating a specific compliance window for centers with large toddler programs.

The two-year documentation retention requirement means ratio tracking is not just a real-time compliance issue: it is an archival one. Software that tracks ratios today but does not retain those records in an exportable format leaves centers exposed when DCCECE requests historical documentation during an audit. The question is not just whether the software tracks ratios, but whether it keeps those records accessible for two years.

## Subsidy billing through CCAP and DCCECE

Arkansas's Child Care Assistance Program is CCDF-funded and administered by DHS DCCECE. Centers billing CCAP submit attendance records to verify care, and payments flow through DCCECE on a monthly schedule.

Attendance records serve two roles: they document care for billing purposes and satisfy the two-year retention requirement for licensing audits. A gap in attendance documentation is both a billing risk and a licensing risk. Centers need software that produces complete, date-stamped attendance records and retains them in accessible form long after the billing cycle closes.

## Seasonal enrollment patterns

Summer enrollment drops when school-age children leave before/after school programs. Centers in Little Rock and Fort Smith follow patterns typical of mid-sized southern cities: a late-May dip and a September surge. Northwest Arkansas centers near Walmart campuses may see less seasonal variation, given the year-round employment patterns of the region's corporate workforce.

Infant and toddler enrollment remains relatively stable year-round. Centers that segment enrollment data by age group can distinguish between seasonal fluctuations in school-age programs and the underlying demand for infant and toddler slots, which helps with staffing and capacity planning.

## What Arkansas directors should ask software vendors

Three questions worth asking before committing to any platform:

Does the software retain ratio documentation for at least two years in a format retrievable for a DCCECE audit? Arkansas's two-year retention requirement is specific. Ask the vendor how records are stored after the billing cycle closes, and whether they remain accessible if you cancel or change plans.

Does it track ratios throughout the operating day, or only at check-in and check-out? Rule 016.06.12 requires continuous ratio documentation. Ask to see what a mid-day ratio report looks like for a specific historical date: not just a screenshot of today's dashboard.

Can it export attendance records in the format DCCECE accepts for CCAP billing? Verify this with your DCCECE contact before committing. Format requirements can change, and "we support attendance exports" is not the same as "our export matches what DCCECE accepts."
