## Wyoming childcare licensing overview

Wyoming has approximately 200 licensed childcare establishments as of 2024, spread across a state with fewer than 600,000 residents. The Department of Family Services regulates centers under Wyoming Rules and Regulations Chapter 3, covering staffing ratios, staff qualifications, physical environment, and recordkeeping. Wyoming is one of the smallest childcare markets in the country by establishment count.

Small market size doesn't simplify the compliance picture. Wyoming centers often serve combined age groups in single classrooms: not because it's preferred, but because the enrollment numbers don't support separate infant, toddler, and preschool rooms. A center with 12 children spread across three age groups is running multiple ratio requirements simultaneously in the same space. Tracking those ratios manually is where documentation errors accumulate.

## Staff-to-child ratios and what they mean for software

Wyoming's ratio structure covers six age bands: infants (0-12 months) at 1:4, toddlers (13-24 months) at 1:5, 2-year-olds at 1:7, 3-year-olds at 1:12, 4 years and older at 1:15, and school-age at 1:20. The 3-year-old ratio of 1:12 and the 4-year-old ratio of 1:15 give Wyoming somewhat more flexible preschool ratios than neighboring states like Idaho and Montana.

The challenge in Wyoming is the mixed-age classroom. When a center runs a combined 2-3 year room, the governing ratio is 1:7 for all children in that room as long as any 2-year-old is present. Software that tracks each child's age group against the classroom's enrolled ages, and alerts when a composition change affects the applicable ratio, catches what manual tracking misses.

## Subsidy billing through CCAP and DFS

Wyoming's Child Care Assistance Program (CCAP) is administered by the Department of Family Services under CCDF funding. Families apply through their local DFS office, and eligible families receive subsidies paid to approved providers. The billing relationship is with DFS, and centers submit attendance documentation to support reimbursement.

Wyoming's small center environment means many programs bill a mix of subsidy and private-pay families. Managing two billing streams with different documentation requirements, DFS attendance records for CCAP families, standard invoices for private-pay, adds administrative overhead. Centers that track attendance and billing in the same system reduce reconciliation work at the end of each billing period.

## Seasonal enrollment patterns

Wyoming's energy economy creates enrollment volatility that most states don't experience. Casper and other oil-producing regions see enrollment rise during energy booms when workers with young children relocate for jobs, and fall during downturns when those families move on. This pattern makes stable enrollment forecasting difficult for centers in energy-dependent communities.

Summer enrollment dips when school-age children leave programs, with the September rebound following the school-year start. Wyoming centers with before/after school care rely on that September recovery to restore revenue after a slow summer. Centers tracking enrollment by age group can project the seasonal pattern and time any hiring or staffing decisions accordingly.

## What Wyoming directors should ask software vendors

Three questions before committing to any platform:

Does the software track ratios by age group throughout the day in mixed-age classrooms: applying the most restrictive ratio when children of different ages share a room? Wyoming licensing inspections under Ch. 3 review the full operating day, and mixed-age rooms are where documentation gaps most often appear.

Can it generate separate attendance reports for CCAP subsidy billing and private-pay invoicing from the same attendance data? Directors managing both billing streams need those outputs without double entry.

If a DFS inspection requests attendance records from the past two years, how quickly can you produce them and in what format? Historical access is a regulatory requirement, not an optional feature.
