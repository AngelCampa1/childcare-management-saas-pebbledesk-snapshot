## Nebraska childcare licensing overview

Nebraska has approximately 600 licensed childcare establishments as of 2024, concentrated in Omaha, Lincoln, and Bellevue, with smaller markets in Grand Island and other regional centers. The Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services licenses childcare centers under 479 NAC 3: a regulatory framework covering staffing ratios, physical environment, staff qualifications, and recordkeeping.

Nebraska's ratio requirements have a distinctive feature that sets them apart from neighboring states: the 12-24 month toddler range is subdivided into two separate age bands with different ratios. Children who are 13-18 months old fall under the 1:4 ratio (the same as infants). Children who are 19-23 months old fall under the more relaxed 1:6 ratio. That distinction may seem minor, but it means a center must track each toddler's precise age in months to correctly classify them for ratio compliance purposes: not just their classroom assignment.

For center directors, DHHS licensing focuses on documented ratio compliance at the age-band level. A toddler classroom with children ranging from 15 to 22 months may have some children under the 1:4 requirement and others under the 1:6 requirement, and that distinction needs to be reflected in ratio documentation.

## Staff-to-child ratios and what they mean for software

Nebraska 479 NAC 3 ratios step from 1:4 for infants through 1:13 for children four and older. The distinctive feature is the toddler split: 13-18 months at 1:4 and 19-23 months at 1:6. Most states use a single toddler ratio across the 12-24 month range. Nebraska does not.

Software that assigns ratio requirements by classroom rather than by individual child age will misclassify toddlers who cross the 18-month threshold mid-year. A child who was correctly counted under 1:4 in January may need to be recounted under 1:6 in February when they turn 19 months, and software that does not update that automatically creates a compliance gap that DHHS inspectors will find.

The ratio tracking requirement is also continuous. Mid-day transitions, teacher breaks, and early pickups all affect the ratio count throughout the operating day.

## Subsidy billing through Child Care Subsidy and DHHS

Nebraska's Child Care Subsidy is CCDF-funded and administered by DHHS Child Care. Centers submit attendance records to verify care, and payments flow through DHHS monthly.

Nebraska's age-band precision requirement applies to billing as well as licensing compliance. If a subsidy-funded child is miscategorized in attendance records due to incorrect age-band assignment, that creates a billing discrepancy that DHHS may flag during reconciliation. Software that correctly tracks each child's current age in months and assigns them to the correct ratio category automatically is more defensible than a system that requires manual age-band updates.

## Seasonal enrollment patterns

Nebraska summer enrollment drops when school-age children leave before/after school programs. Omaha and Lincoln follow typical Great Plains patterns: a late-May dip and a September surge for before/after care. Infant and toddler enrollment is relatively stable year-round.

One nuance: centers with large toddler programs see ratio categories shift throughout the year as children age out of the 13-18 month band into the 19-23 month band. A center that tracks enrollment tightly by child age can anticipate these shifts and plan staffing adjustments before they become ratio compliance problems.

## What Nebraska directors should ask software vendors

Three questions worth asking before committing to any platform:

Does the software track each child's current age in months and automatically update their ratio category when they cross the 18-month threshold into Nebraska's 19-23 month band? This is Nebraska-specific. Most general-purpose childcare software uses a single toddler ratio and will not handle the 13-18 versus 19-23 month distinction correctly.

Does it track ratios continuously throughout the operating day, or only at check-in and check-out? DHHS inspectors review mid-day documentation, not just bookend logs.

Can it export attendance records in the format DHHS accepts for Child Care Subsidy billing? Ask to see the export and compare it against your DHHS contact's current submission requirements before committing.
