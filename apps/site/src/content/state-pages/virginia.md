## Virginia childcare licensing overview

Virginia has approximately 3,000 licensed childcare establishments, with the largest concentration in Northern Virginia's DC suburbs, followed by Richmond, Virginia Beach, and Roanoke. The Department of Education's Division of Child Care and Early Childhood Development licenses childcare centers under 8 VAC 20-780: covering staffing ratios, staff qualifications, physical environment, and recordkeeping requirements.

For center directors, DCCL licensing comes down to documented ratio compliance and clean attendance records. Licensing inspections review staff logs, attendance documentation, and ratio records. Documentation gaps are findings, regardless of whether ratios were actually maintained in practice.

Virginia's childcare market has a characteristic that most states do not: significant military family presence in Northern Virginia and Hampton Roads. Mid-year enrollment transitions tied to military relocations are common in these markets, which means software needs to handle mid-month enrollment changes cleanly: both for billing and for licensing documentation.

## Staff-to-child ratios and what they mean for software

Virginia 8 VAC 20-780 ratios step by age group from 1:4 for infants to 1:18 for school-age children. The ratio requirement at 3 and 4 years old, both 1:8, is tighter than many comparable states, which affects classroom staffing math for preschool programs.

Ratio compliance is continuous. A teacher covering a break, a child transferred between classrooms, afternoon pickup thinning a group: the ratio obligation does not pause. Software that records only arrival and departure captures the boundaries of the day but not the transitions.

During a DCCL inspection, auditors review whether the center can document ratios throughout the operating day for any date in the inspection window. A center with accurate check-in and check-out logs but no room-level ratio tracking for midday periods has a documentation gap.

## Subsidy billing through Virginia CCSP

Virginia's Child Care Subsidy Program is administered by the Department of Social Services through local departments. Virginia has 95 counties and independent cities, each with its own DSS office managing CCSP for families in that jurisdiction. The result is that billing and attendance documentation requirements are locally administered: not uniform across the state.

This structure matters for software selection. A center in Fairfax County and a center in Roanoke City may have different submission requirements from their respective local DSS offices. Before choosing software, contact your local DSS office, understand their attendance documentation requirements, and verify the software can produce compatible records.

Attendance-based billing means your attendance records are your billing documentation. Errors in attendance data translate to billing discrepancies. Centers that reconcile attendance records manually at month-end carry audit risk that centers with automated reconciliation tools reduce.

## Seasonal enrollment patterns

Northern Virginia and Richmond centers with school-age programs see enrollment shifts in June as before/after school care drops off. September brings a corresponding surge. Centers that track classroom-level enrollment can plan staffing adjustments ahead of these transitions rather than reacting to them.

The military mobility factor in Northern Virginia and Hampton Roads creates mid-year enrollment changes that are less predictable than the seasonal school-year patterns. A family receiving PCS orders can need childcare enrollment changed on short notice. Centers in these markets benefit from software that handles mid-month enrollment changes and billing adjustments cleanly, with records that remain audit-ready through the transition.

Infant and toddler enrollment is year-round and heavily waitlisted in Northern Virginia's dense markets. That segment drives consistent revenue independent of the school calendar.

## What software needs to handle in Virginia

Virginia-licensed centers need four capabilities before evaluating anything else:

Continuous ratio tracking by classroom throughout the operating day under 8 VAC 20-780 standards. Point-in-time check-in logs are not sufficient documentation for DCCL inspections.

Attendance records in a format your local DSS office will accept for CCSP billing. Because Virginia administers CCSP locally, this requires direct confirmation with your DSS office: not an assumption based on what worked in another state or jurisdiction.

Clean mid-month enrollment change handling for the military-transition markets, with audit-ready records that reflect the change date accurately in attendance and billing history.

Historical record access for the full licensing inspection window. DCCL inspections can reach back to the previous inspection date. Centers need to retrieve accurate records from that period on demand.
