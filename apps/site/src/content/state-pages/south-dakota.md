## South Dakota childcare licensing overview

South Dakota has approximately 400 licensed childcare establishments as of 2024, concentrated in Sioux Falls and Rapid City, with smaller programs across Aberdeen and rural communities. The Department of Social Services licenses centers under Administrative Rules of South Dakota Ch. 67:42:01, covering staffing ratios, staff qualifications, physical environment, and recordkeeping requirements.

South Dakota's western geography adds a layer of complexity not present in most states: centers near reservation areas may serve families enrolled in both the state Child Care Assistance program and tribal CCDF programs. Tribal governments, including the Oglala Sioux Tribe, Rosebud Sioux Tribe, and others, operate separate CCDF grant programs for eligible families. A center near a reservation boundary may have families billing through DSS and families billing through a tribal CCDF program simultaneously.

## Staff-to-child ratios and what they mean for software

ARSD Ch. 67:42:01 sets ratios across six age groups: infants (0-12 months) at 1:5, toddlers (13-24 months) at 1:7, 2-year-olds at 1:8, 3-year-olds at 1:10, children 4 years and older at 1:13, and school-age at 1:20. South Dakota's infant ratio of 1:5 is somewhat more permissive than neighboring North Dakota's 1:4, but the toddler ratio of 1:7 matches common regional standards.

Mixed-age classrooms, common in smaller South Dakota centers, require tracking which ratio governs. When children from multiple age categories share a classroom, the ratio for the youngest child present applies. Software that identifies the governing ratio based on the youngest enrolled child in each room prevents the documentation error of applying a preschool ratio to a room that still contains a toddler.

## Subsidy billing through DSS and tribal CCDF programs

South Dakota's Child Care Assistance (CCA) program is administered by DSS under CCDF funding. Eligible families apply through DSS, and subsidies are paid directly to licensed providers. Centers submit attendance documentation to support reimbursement. For most South Dakota centers, this is a straightforward state billing relationship.

Centers near reservation areas face a more complex situation. Tribal CCDF programs operate under separate federal grants administered by tribal governments, not through DSS. Each tribal CCDF program has its own eligibility criteria, payment schedules, and documentation requirements. A center serving families from both DSS CCA and a tribal CCDF program is managing two billing relationships with different reporting formats: ideally from the same underlying attendance data.

## Seasonal enrollment patterns

South Dakota's school year drives enrollment patterns in Sioux Falls and Rapid City similarly to other Midwest states: summer dip when school-age children exit licensed programs, September surge when before/after school care fills back up. Centers with significant school-age enrollment depend on the September recovery to offset the summer revenue gap.

Western South Dakota sees additional enrollment patterns tied to the agricultural and tourism economy around the Black Hills. Rapid City's tourism season affects some families' schedules, and agricultural employment in rural counties can shift enrollment timing in ways that don't match the standard school-year calendar.

## What South Dakota directors should ask software vendors

Three questions before committing to any platform:

Does the software track ratios by age group throughout the day and apply the correct governing ratio when multiple age groups share a classroom? DSS licensing inspections under Ch. 67:42:01 review continuous documentation, and mixed-age room errors are among the most common findings.

Can it generate separate attendance reports for DSS CCA billing and tribal CCDF billing from the same attendance records? Centers near reservation areas need dual-billing output without double data entry.

If a DSS audit requests attendance records spanning two or three years, how quickly can you retrieve them and in what format? Historical access should be immediate and complete.
