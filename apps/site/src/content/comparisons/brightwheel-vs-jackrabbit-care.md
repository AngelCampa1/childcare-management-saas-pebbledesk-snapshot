## Different program types, different software needs

Brightwheel and Jackrabbit Care get compared because they both serve programs that work with children. They serve fundamentally different program types.

Brightwheel is built for licensed childcare centers: state-licensed daycares, preschools, and after-school programs. These programs track staff-to-child ratios, hold state licenses, face unannounced inspections, and often bill subsidy agencies for a portion of their enrollment.

Jackrabbit Care is built for class-based programs: gymnastics studios, swim schools, dance academies, and enrichment programs. These programs sell class sessions, manage registrations, and collect fees per class or semester. They typically don't hold state childcare licenses or face CCDF billing requirements.

For a licensed childcare director evaluating management software, Jackrabbit Care is out of scope. The comparison is useful primarily as a clarification.

## Pricing

Jackrabbit Care charges $69 per location per month for 1-5 locations. Multi-location operators with 6-20 locations pay $49 per location per month. QuickBooks integration is available. The pricing model is simple and predictable.

Brightwheel's pricing is enrollment-tiered and not published. Brightwheel requires a written quote, so directors need current terms before budgeting. Payment service fees are program-specific and can add meaningful monthly cost on top of the subscription, so directors should confirm card and bank-transfer terms in writing.

## Where Brightwheel falls short for licensed programs

For licensed childcare directors who do evaluate Brightwheel, the compliance gaps are worth knowing before signing an annual contract.

Brightwheel has no real-time ratio alerts. Directors who manage state-required staff-to-child ratios throughout the day, including nap times, outdoor play, and staff transitions, track ratios manually alongside the software.

Brightwheel produces attendance records, but not in the formats state licensing officers expect during audits. Directors typically export and reformat data manually before inspections.

Subsidy agencies cannot pay through Brightwheel directly. CCDF and DHS voucher reconciliation requires supplemental spreadsheet work each month.

Public review complaints about billing timing and support responsiveness are a directional signal that these gaps create real operational friction for directors running licensed programs.

Jackrabbit Care is not relevant for licensed childcare directors. Brightwheel is relevant but has documented compliance gaps.
