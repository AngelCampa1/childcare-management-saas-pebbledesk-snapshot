## Pricing comparison

Neither Brightwheel nor Sandbox lists pricing publicly.

Brightwheel requires a sales quote for subscription pricing. Payment service fees are program-specific and add on top of the subscription.

Sandbox's pricing requires a sales contact. The platform does not require a long-term contract, which reduces commitment risk compared to platforms with annual-only options.

For directors comparing both without published prices, plan a sales conversation with each before you can put numbers side by side.

## What each platform does well

Brightwheel has the larger market presence and the broader feature set. Billing, enrollment management, waitlist tools, parent messaging, and daily reports are all in one platform. For centers that want a single tool covering parent communication and tuition collection, Brightwheel delivers both.

Sandbox's advantage is simpler onboarding and a cleaner interface. Directors who have struggled with the UX of older platforms, Procare's desktop-era interface is a common comparison point, often find Sandbox easier to adopt. The parent app is polished, invoicing is included, and the learning curve is lower.

Both platforms have real value for the use cases they were built for.

## Where both fall short for licensed programs

The shared compliance gap is the same regardless of which platform you are evaluating.

**Ratio tracking.** Both Brightwheel and Sandbox lack continuous staff-to-child ratio monitoring. State licensing rules require maintaining ratios throughout the operating day: not just at morning check-in. When a licensing officer walks in and asks for ratio records at 10:15 a.m. on a specific date, neither platform has a system-generated answer. Directors on both platforms maintain manual ratio logs alongside the software.

**Subsidy reconciliation.** Brightwheel has subsidy management features, but directors billing CCDF or DHS voucher programs consistently report that the reconciliation output does not match what state agencies expect: manual export and reformatting is the common workaround. Sandbox's billing is designed for private-pay invoicing, while CCDF billing requires additional manual steps. Neither has a purpose-built subsidy workflow.

**Audit-ready documentation.** Brightwheel's reports were designed for parents. Sandbox's records were designed for daily operational needs. Licensing inspections require a different output: time-stamped attendance, room-level ratio logs, and billing records that match what was submitted to the subsidy agency. Neither platform generates this without manual work.

**Offline access.** Both require an internet connection. A licensing inspection during a connectivity interruption leaves directors on either platform without access to records.

## The compliance gap

Both platforms share the same root problem: they were built to manage the center's relationship with parents and families, not its relationship with state licensing agencies and subsidy billing departments.

The metrics that matter for parent-facing software: daily engagement, parent satisfaction, communication frequency: are different from the metrics that matter for licensing compliance: ratio documentation completeness, subsidy claim accuracy, audit response time.

Directors using either Brightwheel or Sandbox as their sole system typically discover this gap during a licensing audit or a rejected subsidy claim.

## Verdict

Brightwheel fits larger centers that prioritize parent engagement, need full billing tools, and can absorb the per-child pricing as enrollment grows. Sandbox fits smaller programs that want a modern, lower-friction platform and primarily serve private-pay families.
