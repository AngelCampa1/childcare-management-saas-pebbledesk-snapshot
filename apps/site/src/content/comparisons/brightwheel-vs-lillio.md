## Pricing comparison

Brightwheel's enrollment-tiered pricing is not fully published. Getting an accurate quote requires a demo call.

Lillio publishes no pricing. Larger centers should ask for a full written quote that includes all children, users, and contract terms. Lillio is Canadian-headquartered, so US centers should also confirm currency and billing terms before signing.

Neither platform publishes setup fees. Both target annual contracts.

## Features that matter

Brightwheel's main advantage for US centers is market penetration. With 50,000+ programs, the parent-facing app is widely recognized: parents who used it at a previous center already know it. The billing and payment workflow is built for US payment systems.

Lillio's advantage is curriculum documentation and learning observations. The platform grew out of HiMama, which focused on linking daily observations to curriculum frameworks. For programs following Reggio, HighScope, or Creative Curriculum, Lillio's documentation tools are more developed than Brightwheel's.

Daily reports, parent messaging, photo sharing, and basic attendance tracking work similarly in both platforms.

## Where each falls short

Brightwheel's operational gaps for compliance-heavy centers are billing timing, no offline mode, and a calendar that doesn't handle multi-room staff scheduling. There is no integration with state subsidy systems.

Lillio's problems are app performance and US regulatory fit. Centers should test performance on the devices teachers actually use before signing. The platform reflects Canadian provincial licensing frameworks: "pedagogical documentation" and "learning stories" are prominent; ratio tracking and subsidy reconciliation tools are not. Pricing requires a sales quote, so directors should model cost at current enrollment and expected growth.

## The compliance gap

Both platforms are built to satisfy parents. Neither is built to satisfy a state licensing officer.

A US state inspector wants specific documentation: room-by-room child-to-staff ratios throughout the operating day, sign-in and sign-out times with parent signatures, incident reports with required fields complete, and subsidy billing records that match attendance logs. Each US state has its own required formats.

Brightwheel produces attendance records but not in audit-ready format for most states. Lillio's reporting reflects Canadian provincial requirements. Directors on either platform maintain paper backup records for licensing compliance: which defeats the point of going paperless.

For US programs billing CCDF, DHS vouchers, or Head Start, subsidy reconciliation is the biggest gap. Both platforms require directors to export data and manually prepare billing claims. A center with multiple funding streams and 15-20 subsidy children can spend 3-5 hours per month on reconciliation that should be automated.

## Verdict

Brightwheel is the more practical choice for most US centers. Its US focus, larger user base, and payment infrastructure are real advantages over a Canadian platform.

Lillio can make sense if curriculum documentation and learning observations are central to your program's identity and you can absorb the pricing opacity at scale.

Neither platform was built for the compliance and subsidy workflows that US licensed centers deal with daily.
