## Two different approaches to childcare software

Procare and Lillio (formerly HiMama) are often evaluated by the same childcare directors, but they were built around different core assumptions about what software should do for a childcare program.

Procare was built around the billing problem: how to manage tuition, subsidy vouchers, family accounts, and co-pays for large US childcare centers. It has decades of billing depth. The legacy of that history shows in the interface; it feels like it was built for the operational environment of the early 2000s, which it was.

Lillio was built around the documentation problem: how to help educators record learning observations, plan curriculum, and communicate with parents about child development. It originated in Canada and rebranded from HiMama as it expanded its product scope. Its venture funding reflects investor confidence in the parent engagement and curriculum documentation market.

## What changed at Procare in 2024

Enterprise ownership changes the incentives around Procare's roadmap and support model.

What this means practically for Procare users: directors should confirm whether product roadmap decisions still reflect childcare director feedback cycles. Mature vertical software businesses often optimize for margin, which means support investment, new feature velocity, and pricing structures should be verified before signing.

Procare's billing depth is real. The acquisition doesn't eliminate that. But directors making multi-year platform commitments should understand who is making roadmap decisions.

## Lillio's Canadian roots in US licensing context

Lillio's strengths are in curriculum documentation and parent communication. Its learning observation tools, daily report sharing, and parent app are well-designed for the educational documentation role.

The challenge for US directors is that Lillio's compliance infrastructure was built for Canadian childcare regulations. The licensing, ratio, and subsidy documentation requirements in US states vary materially from Canadian provinces. Directors relying on Lillio for state audit documentation should verify that export formats meet their specific state inspector requirements; this is not guaranteed.

## The gap both platforms leave

Neither Procare nor Lillio generates reports formatted for US state licensing inspections out of the box. Neither has real-time staff-to-child ratio alerts. Neither automates CCDF reconciliation for programs that bill multiple subsidy agencies monthly.

Directors on both platforms typically run parallel documentation systems; spreadsheets, paper logs, or manual exports to cover audit preparation. That's a solvable problem, but it's worth knowing before choosing a platform.

## Verdict

Procare is the choice for large US centers with complex subsidy billing structures that can absorb the learning curve and live with enterprise ownership dynamics. Lillio is the choice for programs that prioritize curriculum documentation and parent communication, especially those with Canadian connections or standards-based assessment requirements.
