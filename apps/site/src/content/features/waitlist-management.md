## The problem: Waitlists fall apart when family details live outside enrollment

Most centers maintain some form of a waitlist. The problem is that those lists often live
in spreadsheets, paper binders, or email inboxes. When a spot opens, the director has to
reconstruct the child's age, family contacts, and subsidy context from scattered intake
records.

PebbleDesk keeps the waitlist record inside the same enrollment system as active
children. The director still owns the decision and outreach, but the record is already
in the right place.

## The PebbleDesk solution: Keep prospective child records ready for director review

**Child and guardian details.** Waitlisted children use the same child and guardian
record structure as enrolled children, so contact information and pickup context do not
have to be recreated later.

**Date of birth and age group context.** The child's date of birth and age group stay
attached to the record, which helps the director review whether a future opening is
likely to fit.

**Enrollment status.** A waitlisted child is clearly separate from an active child.
PebbleDesk prevents classroom placement for waitlisted children so rosters, ratios, and
capacity counts do not treat prospective enrollment as current attendance.

**Subsidy context.** Directors can keep subsidy-related context in the family record
before enrollment, including details that may affect billing setup or subsidy review
later.

## Converting a spot into an enrollment

When a family accepts a spot, the director updates the child's enrollment status and
completes the normal enrollment workflow. PebbleDesk keeps that process explicit because
classroom assignment, billing setup, and compliance records should not change
automatically without director review.

This is intentionally not a CRM automation engine. If a center needs automated waitlist
campaigns, hold-fee workflows, or priority scoring, those should be managed in a
dedicated enrollment CRM or a manual director process alongside PebbleDesk.

## Using waitlist data for planning

Directors who keep waitlisted children in PebbleDesk have cleaner source records for
capacity conversations. The same system distinguishes active, waitlist, and withdrawn
status, which makes it easier to avoid mixing current enrollment with future demand.

## What directors should review next

A waitlist should help a director make a thoughtful enrollment decision, not force a family into an automated funnel. PebbleDesk keeps prospective child details close to [enrollment records](/features/enrollment-records), [parent portal](/features/parent-portal), and classroom planning so the office can review age, guardian contacts, subsidy context, and likely room fit before outreach. The center still owns priority rules, sibling preference, and timing. That matters because a spot is not just an empty seat. It has to match classroom capacity, staff coverage, and the child start date. PebbleDesk keeps the record ready so accepting a family is less likely to start with retyping intake information. It also helps directors separate current enrollment from future demand, which keeps rosters and ratio counts cleaner.
