## What Lillio charges US customers (and why you can't find out without calling)

Lillio does not publish pricing for the US market. Visit their website and you'll find a "Contact Sales" button where a pricing page would normally be. Their Canadian pricing is visible with per-child per-month tiers, but US centers go through a negotiated sales process.

That opacity has real costs beyond the inconvenience of a sales call. You can't compare Lillio's cost to alternatives without committing time to their funnel. Pricing varies based on your negotiating position and center size. There's no published number to hold them accountable to at renewal.

### What's publicly known about their tier structure

Lillio offers multiple tiers, from basic parent communication to fuller center management. Based on Canadian market pricing and product documentation, the structure follows a pattern common to parent-engagement platforms.

An entry tier covers child profiles, daily reports, photos, and parent messaging. A higher tier adds billing, staff scheduling, and curriculum planning. An enterprise tier covers multi-site operators with API access and dedicated support.

The product is built around parent communication. The daily report, the photo feed, the parent app: these are the core features that Lillio (then HiMama) was founded to deliver.

### The US compliance gap

Lillio started in Canada in 2012. The Canadian childcare licensing environment differs from US state-by-state regulations, and the platform's reporting layer reflects its origins. Reports are formatted for parents, not for licensing officers.

US directors running licensed programs face inspections where the documentation requirements are specific: attendance logs with timestamps, staff-to-child ratio records tied to specific time periods, incident reports in a state-prescribed format, subsidy billing records for CCDF or DHS vouchers. Lillio's parent-facing daily sheets don't map to those requirements without manual work.

CCDF subsidy reconciliation, tracking which children have vouchers, what the agency pays versus what parents owe, and producing the documentation for reimbursement, is not a documented Lillio feature. For US programs where subsidy revenue is 30-50% of total income, managing that process outside the platform means spreadsheets, manual entry, and significant director time.

### Hidden costs to factor in

The per-child pricing model scales with enrollment. A center with 40 children pays more than one with 20. Without a published US rate, you won't know how much more until you've completed the sales call. Implementation fees may also apply: onboarding support is typically negotiated as part of the contract.

Annual contracts are standard for US customers. If you sign in January and the platform doesn't work for your audit in March, you're still paying through December.

### How PebbleDesk compares

PebbleDesk publishes pricing: Home at {{plan.home.priceLabel}} for in-home daycares and small programs, Center Starter at {{plan.center_starter.priceLabel}} for licensed centers that need subsidy reconciliation and audit-ready reports. No sales call required to know if it fits your budget.

The reporting layer is built for US state licensing auditors, not parents. Attendance records export in the format inspectors look for. Staff-to-child ratio tracking is automated with alerts, not a manual log you maintain alongside the software. CCDF and DHS voucher billing is a workflow inside the platform, not a spreadsheet you run separately.

Both platforms handle parent communication. The difference is what gets prioritized when the two conflict, and for a director whose license depends on clean audit documentation, that priority matters.

If you're evaluating Lillio, ask their sales team specifically: what does a state licensing audit report look like from your system, and how does CCDF subsidy reconciliation work? The answers will tell you whether the platform was designed for your compliance environment or for something adjacent to it.
