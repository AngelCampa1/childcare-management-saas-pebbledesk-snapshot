## What Brightwheel does well for preschools

Parent communication is Brightwheel's genuine strength. The daily report format, photo sharing, and in-app messaging are well-designed. Parents engage with the app. For a preschool whose enrollment depends on family word-of-mouth and visible signs of quality, Brightwheel's parent experience investment has a measurable return.

Digital enrollment also works well. Moving from paper enrollment packets to online forms, digital signatures, and cloud-stored immunization records is a real operational improvement that Brightwheel handles cleanly.

For preschools in their first year of moving off paper systems, Brightwheel is often the right first step.

## Where Brightwheel falls short as preschools grow

Preschools that have been using Brightwheel for two or more years typically encounter the same set of friction points.

Pricing is the first. A preschool that opened with 25 children and has grown to 60 is paying significantly more than it did at the start. The functionality has not changed proportionally. Brightwheel's enrollment-based pricing model penalizes the growth that the software helped enable.

NAEYC accreditation is the second. Preschools pursuing NAEYC accreditation need to document evidence across ten program standards and maintain a self-study portfolio. Brightwheel was not designed to organize evidence into that framework. Directors working toward NAEYC accreditation describe assembling documentation manually or in a separate system while paying for Brightwheel's parent communication features.

Subsidy billing is the third. Preschools serving children with state childcare vouchers, including CCDF, Pre-K expansion slots, or local subsidy programs, need billing reconciliation reports that match what state agencies request. Brightwheel's subsidy output requires manual reformatting before submission. For a preschool where 30% of families use vouchers, that is a recurring manual task every billing cycle.

## What ratio compliance looks like in a preschool

Preschool room ratios vary by state and by age group. A room of three-year-olds has a different licensed ratio requirement than a room of four-year-olds. When a teacher goes to lunch or a substitute does not arrive on time, that classroom may be temporarily out of ratio.

Brightwheel shows attendance counts, but it does not provide real-time alerts when a classroom's ratio drops below the licensed threshold. A director managing three or four preschool rooms cannot monitor ratio status continuously while handling the rest of the day. PebbleDesk sends alerts when a classroom goes out of ratio so the director can respond before it becomes a licensing issue.
