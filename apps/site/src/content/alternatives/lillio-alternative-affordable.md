## The Lillio Pricing Problem

You're a licensed center director. You have a software budget and a half-hour to evaluate three tools before parent pickup starts. You open Lillio's website, navigate to pricing, and find: request a demo.

That's not an accident. Software companies that don't publish pricing do it because it gives them negotiating control. They learn your budget before you learn their price. For a center director trying to compare tools independently, this is a real barrier.

The time cost of a sales process; demo scheduling, introductory call, follow-up, quote; is measured in hours. If you're evaluating five tools and three of them require demos before you get a number, you've lost a full day before you've made any progress.

## What Lillio Gets Right

Lillio's learning documentation tools are genuinely strong. If your center is required to maintain learning portfolios, development observations, and curriculum documentation for licensing or accreditation purposes, Lillio's tools for that workflow are well-developed.

Parent communication is solid. Daily reports, photos, and messaging in one app covers the parent-facing experience competently.

For centers where the primary software job is learning documentation and parent engagement, not subsidy billing management and ratio compliance; Lillio is a legitimate option once you get through the pricing conversation.

## Where Lillio Falls Short for Compliance-Focused Directors

The directors who contact us most often are not asking about learning portfolios. They're asking about ratio violations, CCAP billing reconciliation, and what happens during a state licensing visit.

Lillio was built from an early childhood education lens; learning, development, documentation. That's valuable, but it's not the same as being built for the compliance-and-operations lens that a licensed center director lives in daily.

Ratio tracking in Lillio supports documentation. It doesn't alert your lead teacher that Room B is one child over ratio when three kids arrive simultaneously at morning drop-off. Subsidy billing is not a primary feature, which means centers with 30-40% voucher-funded enrollment are maintaining separate tracking regardless.
